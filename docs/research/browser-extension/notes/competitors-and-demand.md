# PDF browser extensions: competitors, complaints, built-in viewer gaps, and demand for a local-only option

Sampled 2026-11-09. Store numbers come from WebFetch of the listing pages on that date unless marked otherwise. Budget limits: about 18 tool calls. Safari, Xodo, Smallpdf (official), Lumin, DocHub, PDFgear and Sider listings were not fetched, so they are listed as gaps.

## Q1. The main PDF extensions: numbers, positioning, whether they take over PDF opening, local processing vs upload, business model

### Takeaway
Adobe Acrobat dominates Chrome by two orders of magnitude (351M users). The only notable open-source, local viewer is Mozilla's PDF.js "PDF Viewer" by Rob Wu (1M users, last updated Oct 2024, so it looks lightly maintained). Below those two, the market is SaaS funnels (Kami, Smallpdf, iLovePDF, Foxit) and a long tail of small clones and "private/local" newcomers with almost no users. On Firefox, no PDF viewer extension makes the top 10 results for "pdf", because pdf.js is built in.

### Inferences
- Above the long tail, the only "local, open-source, opens your PDFs" option on Chrome is the PDF.js extension. It has gone more than 2 years without an update and carries opt-out telemetry, which leaves a credible slot open for a maintained, local-only, open-source viewer.
- The other big players (Adobe, Kami, Smallpdf, iLovePDF, Foxit) use the extension as a funnel to a cloud account or subscription. Upload and upsell are their business model, so a privacy-first entrant would not be competing with them on their own terms.
- Firefox is the weakest market for a viewer extension because pdf.js is built in and has editing. Chrome and Edge are where the opportunity is.

### Gaps
- Official Xodo, Smallpdf, Foxit, Sejda, Lumin, DocHub, PDFgear and Sider/"chat with PDF" listings (user counts, permissions) were not fetched. The ratings for Xodo (3.8) and Foxit (2.8) come only from CWS related panels.
- The Chrome listing pages, as fetched, did not show permissions. chrome-stats.com data was not obtained.
- Edge Add-ons and Safari/App Store PDF extensions were not researched.
- Review text (complaint samples) is not rendered in fetched CWS pages. The complaints here come from Adobe's forums instead.

## Q2. Recurring user complaints

### Takeaway
The documented complaints cluster around the incumbent intercepting PDFs and then failing: broken rendering, lost page anchors, double fetches, large files crashing. Next come forced cloud and upsell flows, browsers fighting over the default handler, and the lack of real dark mode.

### Cited Findings
- Hijack-then-fail with Acrobat: PDFs redirect between viewers, render blank, or crash on large files, and disabling the extension fixes it. — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/adobe-acrobat-online-discussions/problem-with-adobe-chrome-extension/m-p/15488584); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/adobe-acrobat-online-discussions/problem-with-adobe-chrome-extension/td-p/15409221/highlight/true)
- Breaks deep links (`#page=`) and one-time download URLs. — [page links bug](https://community.adobe.com/t5/adobe-acrobat-online-discussions/bug-report-acrobat-chrome-extension-does-not-respect-page-specific-pdf-links/td-p/13223193); [double fetch](https://community.adobe.com/t5/adobe-acrobat-online-discussions/adobe-acrobat-chrome-extension-doing-multiple-calls/m-p/15480238)
- Fights over the default handler: users complain Edge keeps reclaiming PDFs from Acrobat, and that there is "absolutely no response to complaints". — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-9/edge-takes-over-pdf-files-where-is-the-best-place-to-raise-my-issue-1250039); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-discussions/microsoft-edge-changes-my-default-settings/m-p/10934982)
- Upsell and paywall resentment: The Register framed Edge's switch as moving to a "pay-to-play" Adobe Acrobat, and an Adobe forum poster says residential users "fall prey and end up paying". — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2023/02/10/microsoft_edge_pdf_acrobat/); [Register forum](https://forums.theregister.com/forum/all/2023/02/10/microsoft_edge_pdf_acrobat/)
- Dark mode: dark-mode extensions can only invert the colours of Chrome's viewer because "of the limitations of Chrome's PDF viewer", and Dark Reader does not work on the canvas. — [DarkPDF listing](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/darkpdf/cfemcmeknmapecneeeaajnbhhgfgkfhp?hl=en); [Dark Reader discussion #12493](https://www.github.com/darkreader/darkreader/discussions/12493); [DEV post](https://dev.to/liuhao04/why-most-pdf-dark-mode-chrome-extensions-do-nothing-on-a-web-pdf-on5)
- Local files: the PDF.js extension needs a manual "Allow access to file URLs" toggle, and form-POST PDFs fail. — [CWS PDF Viewer](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/pdf-viewer/oemmndcbldboiebfnladdacbdfmadadm)
- Over-broad permissions: PDF Toolbox requested all-sites access "to scan every open tab for PDFs" when activeTab would have been enough. — [Palant](https://palant.info/2023/05/16/malicious-code-in-pdf-toolbox-extension/)

### Inferences
- Respecting page anchors and one-time URLs, never double-fetching, coping with huge files and offering a one-click way back to the native viewer are all differentiators that can be shown directly against Acrobat's documented bugs.
- Asking only for minimal permissions (activeTab and declarativeNetRequest instead of `<all_urls>` where possible) is itself a trust signal after PDF Toolbox.

### Gaps
- Reddit and HN threads were not reached directly; search returned mostly Adobe forum and mirror pages. Complaints about annotations not saving were not found with sources.

## Q3. Notable incidents

### Takeaway
The defining incident is PDF Toolbox in 2023: 2M users, a 4.2 rating and a hidden remote-code-injection backdoor. It led to removals across about 32 to 34 extensions with 75M combined installs. That makes "PDF extension" a known malware disguise, which a verifiably open-source, local-only product can position against.

### Cited Findings
- PDF Toolbox had 2M+ users and a 4.2 rating. Its code was disguised as an "extension API wrapper" and waited 24h before letting serasearchtop[.]com inject JS into every page. The code had been present for at least a year, and Google left it up after the first report. The same code was found in at least 11 other extensions. — [Wladimir Palant, 2023-05-16](https://palant.info/2023/05/16/malicious-code-in-pdf-toolbox-extension/)
- Google then removed about 32 extensions with 75M combined installs. TechRadar puts the count at 34. — [BleepingComputer, 2023-06-02](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/malicious-chrome-extensions-with-75m-installs-removed-from-web-store/?web_view=true); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/a-host-of-malicious-google-chrome-extensions-with-75-million-installs-have-been-removed)
- Adobe silently installed its Chrome extension with Reader updates, with telemetry on by default. — [gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/?p=129704)
- Search-hijack "converter" add-ons with PDF-themed names sit near the top of AMO's PDF search (100K+ users each, rated 2.3 to 3.0). — [AMO search](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/search/?q=pdf&sort=users&type=extension)

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 PDF-extension incident turned up, and no confirmed case of a PDF extension being acquired and then monetised; the searches were limited.

## Q4. Gaps in the built-in viewers as of 2026

### Takeaway
The built-in viewers have closed the basic annotation gap. Chrome added highlights, notes and signatures in Chrome 145 (Feb 2026), and Firefox has had a full editor since Fx106+. Edge is now Adobe's engine with upsells. What is still missing across browsers: a real dark or reflow reading mode (Chrome and Firefox both lack a toggle), performance on huge files, and a viewer free of vendor cloud funnels (Chrome pushes Drive, Edge pushes Adobe).

### Cited Findings
- **Chrome**: PDF annotation (highlight, notes, signatures) and "Save to Drive" (into a "Saved from Chrome" folder) launched 2026-02-19 and were broadly available from Chrome 145. — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/02/19/chrome-split-view-pdf/); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/02/19/chrome-split-view-pdf-annotations/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/chrome-split-view-and-pdf-annotations-3642504/)
- **Chrome**: no native dark mode for PDFs. The only workaround is the experimental Force Dark flag, and extensions are limited to colour inversion. — [Brave community](https://community.brave.com/t/add-dark-mode-to-native-brave-pdf-reader/519556); [DEV post](https://dev.to/liuhao04/why-most-pdf-dark-mode-chrome-extensions-do-nothing-on-a-web-pdf-on5); [MS Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/discussions/dark-mode-pdf/m-p/2442928)
- **Edge**: switched to the Adobe Acrobat PDF engine from 2023. The legacy engine's retirement slipped from early 2025 to early 2026 to June 2026 and finally to "early September 2026" (Message Center MC1072406, updated 2026-05-18). Microsoft claims "full feature parity". — [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/2692717/microsoft-confirms-plan-to-kill-its-legacy-pdf-reader-in-edge-soon.html); [Windows Latest](https://windowslatest.com/2025/04/16/microsoft-edge-will-auto-update-pdf-to-adobe-engine-wont-kill-off-legacy-pdf-until-2026); [MC1072406 diff](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1072406/compare/2025-11-14T21-39-36-033Z/2026-05-18T16-51-56-293Z); [heise](https://heise.de/-10356905); [MS Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftedgeinsider/microsoft-edge-and-adobe-partner-to-improve-the-pdf-experience/3733481)
- **Firefox**: the built-in editor supports text, ink, highlight (Fx125 experiment, default in 126), images with alt text, signatures (simple annotations, "not legally binding") and comments. Third-party articles say Fx150 added page reorder and delete. — [Mozilla SUMO](https://support.mozilla.org/kb/view-pdf-files-firefox); [Bugzilla 1867513](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1867513); [firefox.com PDF editor](https://www.firefox.com/features/pdf-editor/); [Techwalla on Fx150](https://www.techwalla.com/13779414/firefox-150-pdf-editor-explained-what-it-does-and-doesnt-do) (not verified against Mozilla release notes)
- **Firefox**: no dark-mode toggle. `pdfjs.viewerCssTheme` was removed in Fx121, and `resistFingerprinting` forces light mode. Users keep asking for more editing on Mozilla Connect ("Improve PDF editing functionality"). — [SUMO 1540062](https://support.mozilla.org/el/questions/1540062); [pdf.js #12290](https://www.github.com/mozilla/pdf.js/issues/12290); [pdf.js PR #17293](https://github.com/mozilla/pdf.js/pull/17293); [Mozilla Connect idea](https://connect.mozilla.org/t5/ideas/improve-pdf-editing-functionality/idc-p/82710)

### Inferences
- With Chrome shipping free annotation, "annotate in browser" is no longer a selling point. The remaining wedges are reading quality (dark and reflow modes, accessibility, huge-file speed), trust (no cloud funnel, open source) and consistency (the same viewer across Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari).
- Edge users now get an Adobe-branded viewer by default. A neutral alternative may appeal to them, but Edge Add-ons demand was not measured.

### Gaps
- Safari and Preview gaps were not researched. Neither was Chrome's Skia/OOPIF viewer migration, nor accessibility, reflow, tabs or find quality in each viewer. No crbug entry for PDF dark mode was located.

## Q5. Evidence of demand for an open-source, local-only PDF extension

### Takeaway
There is a steady trickle of supply: Show HN posts and new CWS and AMO listings branded "private/local/open source". There is little proof of demand at scale, though. The new local-only entrants have tiny user counts (OpenPDF has 26 users), and the only open-source viewer with scale is PDF.js at 1M. The niche exists, but nobody has won it, and distribution rather than features looks like the hard part.

### Cited Findings
- A Show HN post presented an open-sourced "privacy first PDF Chrome Extension" (PDFTool) whose premise was "your PDFs never leave your device". It was seen via an HN mirror, so the original thread was not verified. — [HN mirror](https://frontpage-app.vercel.app/show/47563103)
- OpenPDF calls itself "100% private, client-side… No uploads, no tracking, no servers" and open source (no repo linked). It has **26 users** and no ratings, last updated 2026-05-06. — [CWS OpenPDF](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/openpdf-free-private-edit/eblfbmkkoiphfocgnnknpgljnncglcek)
- LocalPDF advertises a browser extension with 12+ tools, all processed locally, with auditable open source. — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/localpdf/localpdf-browser-extension-now-available-on-chrome-web-store)
- On a Show HN for BreezePDF (an in-browser editor), a commenter raised AGPL licensing of MuPDF, a reminder that engine licensing matters for this category. — [HN mirror](https://next-hackernews-olive.vercel.app/stories/46675231)
- The PDF.js extension has 1M users and 2.7K ratings without marketing, which suggests real demand for a non-Adobe default viewer. — [CWS](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/pdf-viewer/oemmndcbldboiebfnladdacbdfmadadm)

### Inferences
- The positioning "local-only" is crowded with small, unverified claims. Lamina could stand out by making the claim verifiable: a public repo, reproducible builds, minimal permissions, no telemetry. It could also beat PDF.js on speed and huge files, and keep the extension maintained, which PDF.js's has not been since Oct 2024.

### Gaps
- No survey data, Reddit threads or install trends (chrome-stats) quantifying demand were obtained.

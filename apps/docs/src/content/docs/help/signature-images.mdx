---
title: Add a signature image to a PDF
description: Draw, type or upload a picture of your signature, place it on a page, keep it on your device, and see how it differs from a signature that proves who signed.
---

A signature image puts a picture of your handwriting on the page. Anyone can draw, type or paste the same picture, so it doesn't prove who signed. It shows that someone agreed to the document, and for many forms that is all the recipient needs. This page covers the web reader at [app.laminapdf.com](https://app.laminapdf.com).

## Add one

Open the PDF, choose **Fill & Sign** in the mode switcher, then choose **Add a signature image** in the toolbar. You can also run **Add a signature image** from the command palette. The dialog offers four ways to get a signature:

- **Draw:** sign above the line with a mouse, trackpad, pen or finger. Pick black or dark blue ink, **Undo stroke** takes back the last stroke, and **Clear** starts again.
- **Type:** enter your name and choose handwriting, italic or spaced capitals. Each choice previews your name in its own style.
- **Upload:** choose a PNG or JPEG under 10 MB, such as a photo of a signature on white paper. The reader removes the white background and shows the result before you place it.
- **Saved:** pick a signature you kept earlier.

Choose **Place signature**. The picture lands in the middle of the page you are reading. Placing it is one undo step, and the toast has an **Undo** button.

## Choose how it goes on the page

**A movable signature image** is the default. The picture is an annotation, so other PDF readers show it and you can select, move and delete it. A drawn signature can also go on as **ink strokes**, which keeps the lines you drew instead of a picture of them.

**Part of the page** makes the picture part of the page's own content, so every reader prints and shows it. This choice is off when the file has a certification signature, because changing the page would break the certification. The dialog says so.

A file that already carries a digital signature changes when you add a picture, and the signature then reports a change made after signing. The reader asks before it adds one that would break a signature.

## Keep signatures on your device

**Keep in my signatures on this device** is on by default. The browser stores the picture, and nothing is uploaded. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes your saved signatures. The **Saved** tab lists them, and **Remove** deletes one.

## A picture is not a digital signature

A signature image doesn't prove who signed or whether the file changed afterwards. A digital signature does both: it ties a certificate to the exact contents of the file. Signing with a certificate comes in a later update; until then, use a signature image when a recipient needs only a sign of agreement, and keep the original file when you need to prove more.
